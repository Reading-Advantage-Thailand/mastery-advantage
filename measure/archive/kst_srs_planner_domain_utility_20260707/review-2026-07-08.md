## 3. Code quality observations

- **Clear separation of concerns:** The engine consumes a scalar `utility` plus provenance; data-layer composition stays inside the provider.
- **Domain-neutrality preserved:** No domain-specific logic leaks into the core planner beyond the adapter-registered provider.
- **Determinism:** Fuzz is hash-based; tie-breaking is explicit (priority desc, then `nodeId` asc).
- **Ranking-layer dependency well-flagged:** D2 records the exact interface (`UtilitySignal`) the sibling track must adopt.
- **Plan-to-spec minor relocation:** The review-load budget was placed in §10.5 rather than inside §12.7 as the plan originally sketched. This is reasonable because load budgeting is a planner concern, but the plan text should ideally be updated to match; it is not.
- **Approval gap:** Pedagogy review was by owner directive, with constants folded into tech debt.

## 4. Risks or blockers

- **Sibling-track coordination:** The planner track is complete, but the `frequency_semantic_ranking_layer_20260611` track must still adopt the `UtilitySignal` schema and ship the reference English frequency provider. Until then, the utility term is inert for vocabulary domains.
- **Arbitrary thresholds:** The <5% prerequisite-in-edge threshold for sparse-domain detection, the 2-per-group diversity cap, and `loadBudgetFactor=0.8` are not empirically justified in the spec.
- **Review-load projection window:** A 7-day rolling average may be too noisy for weekly classroom schedules; no sensitivity analysis is provided.
- **Pedagogy ratification outstanding:** Term weights (0.35/0.20/0.15/0.10/0.20), the 0.7/0.3 sparse split, and the ±5% fuzz window need human review.
